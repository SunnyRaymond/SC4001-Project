\section{Conclusion}
Few-shot adaptation of frozen CLIP can improve mean accuracy while introducing errors on images that zero-shot CLIP already classifies correctly. On Office-Home, increasing the support set expands the number of corrected errors, but negative flips persist. Confidence gating beats random selection yet adds little over the stronger adapters, and our VRF-style distance weighting is close to its random-set control. Under the evaluated source-only selection protocol, the zero-shot-initialized probe with fixed probability mixing offers a useful accuracy--harm compromise: at four shots, it reaches 83.24\% accuracy with 2.43\% harm. The practical lesson is to report both improvements and regressions, and to establish a strong fixed-mixing baseline before adding sample-wise selection. These findings concern one dataset and one frozen backbone; larger support sets, calibrated probabilities and other representations remain open directions.
